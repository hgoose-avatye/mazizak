# Audio diagnosis — 2026-11-12

Scope: current loopback development app on port 4354. Read-only runtime instrumentation
and PCM analysis; no keyboard, fidget model, source audio or playback code changed.
Evidence: 2026-11-12-evidence.json. Human listening has not been performed.

## Oil King / Boba U4T

Confirmed: different WAV hashes, distinct fetch/cache keys and registration names,
7 Oil King / 17 Boba down samples. Twenty actual Q presses per preset used distinct
AudioBuffer fingerprints across the two presets (zero cross-preset matches).
Both used recorded samples, not the shared synthesized pebble base. Boba's 14 observed
unique buffers in 20 random presses does not imply a 14-sample pool: the chooser only
avoids its two most recent choices, not a complete shuffle cycle.

Signal-level finding: median spectral centroids in the current complete clips are
2579 Hz (Oil King) / 2589 Hz (Boba); median clip RMS -43.1 / -39.2 dBFS. These figures
use full-clip FFT power without a Hann window because these are short transients.
They establish similar spectral balance, not identical sound or perceptual equivalence.
Cross-preset waveform correlation is at most about 0.60, not an identity match.

Both presets randomly pool general down clips, with no independently recorded release
or dedicated space/enter/backspace source. Space falls back to the same down pool.
Thus there are fewer verified behavior/sound differences than the UI's model labels imply.
Oil King's baked 10x compensation changes level, not its spectral character; both
receive the same +6 dB runtime makeup. No local load-error substitution was observed.

Conclusion: no shared-buffer routing bug found. Weak differentiation in these selected
recordings is the leading explanation; source identity/recording conditions and human
A/B assessment remain unresolved. EQ/pitch alone should not be presented as new genuine
Oil King/Boba recordings. Re-evaluate representative original clips under matched
recording/keycap conditions before changing their sound profiles.

## Fidget source/role mismatch

- L01 and L02 press/compress use cheese and mango *mallang* squishy WAVs, not verified
  recordings of cheese/mango slime. L03 uses sherbet-slangi samples, not whipped cream.
- S01 uses generic mallang; S02 butter squishy compression; S03 peach/tangerine pools.
  Their filenames do not establish TPR, PU slow-rise or mochi material authenticity.
- W02 is mint-choco wax reference; C02 is pudding crunch, not a verified sugar-bead recording.
- Installed-package WAV availability was previously described too strongly as verified
  original recordings. It verifies the files exist, not their physical recording provenance.
- 61 selected WAV files contain only 51 unique PCM streams. Apple first_001 equals
  break_001; first_002 equals break_003. Soap and pudding first_001..004 equal their
  corresponding break_001..004. Filename roles alone do not establish different actions.

## Fidget playback defects

1. Press and compress reuse the same pool. A stationary press triggers a second
   compression after about 280 ms, creating another attack rather than continuous material motion.
2. Short compression clips are used as friction/stretch loops with random initial offset,
   a generic low-pass and no authored loop seam/crossfade. This does not preserve a real
   sustained stretch gesture or its event timing.
3. All toys retain common CC0-derived release layers; foam release is a 1.82-second
   processed recover sound in the measured run. It is not an actual foam-rise recording.
   Bubble sounds are arbitrary first-90-ms slices of a compression/knead clip.
4. Per-patch crack events retrigger long crack recordings. In the bounded apple gesture,
   24 one-shots started over roughly 3.7 seconds, reaching seven scheduled overlapping
   one-shots and about -7.0 dB compressor gain reduction. Sugar beads reached seven
   scheduled overlapping one-shots too. This audibly relevant mixing mechanism can alter
   dynamics and texture; the subjective degree still needs listening.
5. Crunch samples are cut to 360 ms regardless of semantic boundaries; peak-only
   normalization cannot match perceived level across sources with different crest factors.
   A source clip may already contain multiple actions, then be replayed once per fragment.

## Correction order

1. Establish raw-versus-runtime A/B listening with per-role source provenance; select
   correct-material, single-action recordings. Mark approximations honestly.
2. Remove duplicate press/compress attacks and unsupported release/bubble layers.
3. Use actual continuous gesture recordings with safe loop boundaries or granular
   event segments; drive sound by displacement/pressure-change energy rather than frame count.
4. Aggregate wax fracture events and limit per-material voice overlap; preserve crisp
   transients with measured headroom, then tune perceived loudness through listening.
5. Validate quality against physical recordings, separately from event-scheduling tests.

Previous 71 fidget / 70 keyboard checks verified functionality and routing. They never
established acoustic realism; those results should not have been used as a quality conclusion.
